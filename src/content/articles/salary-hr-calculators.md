In corporate and manufacturing audits I conduct across commercial enterprises in Dhaka, I frequently inspect four critical metrics that management teams treat as completely separate silos: **salary structures, employee turnover rates, EBITDA margins, and the balance sheet current ratio.**

Human Resources manages compensation, department managers manage turnover, finance directors track EBITDA, and accounting monitors the balance sheet.

Yet, in every underperforming business I have audited, these four numbers form an unbroken causal chain:

> **Underpriced Salaries ──► High Employee Turnover ──► Crushed EBITDA Margin ──► Depleted Current Ratio**

I once audited a medium-sized manufacturing plant where annual staff turnover had climbed to **28%**, well above the industry benchmark of 18%. When our audit team investigated the root cause, it traced directly back to base wages set 12% below market rate for specialized machine operators. 

The founder believed he was saving $90,000 annually on payroll. In reality, the company was quietly spending over **$340,000 in continuous re-hiring costs**—agency recruitment commissions, overtime for remaining operators, and ruined materials during new-hire ramp-up. 

Those hidden costs inflated operating expenses, compressing their EBITDA margin from a healthy 13% down to 6.8%. Because cash was constantly drained to replace workers, their current ratio fell to 1.15, causing their bank to reject a critical equipment expansion loan.

Fix the salary structure → turnover drops → EBITDA expands → working capital stabilizes.

## Key Takeaways: The 4 Interconnected Metrics

- **The Real Cost of Hourly Hires:** Converting an hourly contractor to a salaried employee requires adding a **25% to 35% employer burden** for statutory taxes, healthcare, and leave.
- **The Turnover Blind Spot:** Replacing a single mid-level worker costs **50% to 100% of their annual salary** in combined hard and soft expenses.
- **EBITDA Quality:** EBITDA strips out non-operational financing decisions and taxes, serving as the universal yardstick for enterprise valuation.
- **The Liquidity Floor:** Lenders require a **Current Ratio of 1.5 to 2.0**; allowing turnover or payroll friction to deplete cash below 1.0 triggers immediate loan rejections.

## Metric 1: Hourly to Annual Salary Conversion

Converting an hourly contractor rate into an equitable full-time corporate salary requires factoring in statutory employer taxes and benefits.

### The Universal Conversion Formulas

> **Annual Gross Salary = Hourly Rate × Working Hours Per Week × 52 Weeks**  
> *(Standard 40-hour full-time year = Hourly Rate × 2,080 Hours)*  
> **Reverse Hourly Rate = Annual Gross Salary ÷ 2,080 Hours**

### The Employer Burden Gap: Contractor vs. Full-Time Employee

| Compensation Component | Independent 1099 Contractor | W-2 Full-Time Salaried Hire |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Base Cash Pay | $35.00 / hr ($72,800/yr) | $72,800 / year base |
| Health, Dental & Vision Insurance | $0.00 (Self-funded) | +$9,500 (Employer funded) |
| Paid Time Off (15 Days Vacation/Sick) | $0.00 (Unpaid) | +$4,200 (Paid downtime) |
| Mandatory Employer Payroll Taxes (FICA) | $0.00 (Paid by contractor) | +$5,569 (7.65% match) |
| 401(k) / Pension Match (3%) | $0.00 | +$2,184 (Company match) |
| **Total Real Cost to the Business** | **$72,800 / year** | **$94,253 / year (+29.4% Burden)** |

💡 **Auditor's Takeaway:** A salaried employee earning $72,800 costs the business nearly **$95,000 all-in**. To achieve true cost parity when hiring a contractor who covers their own taxes and insurance, their equivalent billing rate should be **$45.31/hour**, not $35/hour.

## Metric 2: Employee Turnover Rate & Replacement Costs

Most corporate profit and loss (P&L) statements do not have a line item called "Turnover Expense." Instead, turnover quietly drains profitability across multiple cost centers.

### The Turnover Rate Formula

> **Turnover Rate (%) = [Total Departures (Voluntary + Involuntary) ÷ Average Headcount] × 100**  
> *Where Average Headcount = (Employees at Start + Employees at End) ÷ 2*

### The Real Cost to Replace a Departed Employee

| Role Complexity Level | Typical Annual Salary | Replacement Cost Range | Key Cost Drivers |
|---|:---:|:---:|---|
| **Entry-Level / Operational** | $35,000 | **$12,000 – $18,000 (35%–50%)** | Onboarding, supervisor training hours, scrap waste |
| **Mid-Level / Technical / Sales** | $65,000 | **$42,000 – $65,000 (65%–100%)** | Recruiter fees (15–20%), vacant desk downtime |
| **Senior Executive / Specialized** | $120,000+ | **$120,000 – $240,000 (100%–200%)** | Executive headhunters, lost client relationships |

> **Audit Walkthrough:** A 60-person engineering consultancy with an average salary of $70,000 running a 25% turnover rate loses 15 staff members each year. At a conservative $50,000 replacement cost per engineer, the company is burning **$750,000 annually in pure friction**. Reducing turnover to 12% saves **$390,000 in net cash every single year**.

## Metric 3: EBITDA Margin (Operational Cash Generation)

**EBITDA** (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization) strips away capital structure choices, local tax rates, and non-cash accounting adjustments to measure pure operating profitability.

### The EBITDA Formulas

> **EBITDA = Net Income + Interest Expense + Income Taxes + Depreciation + Amortization**  
> **EBITDA Margin (%) = (EBITDA ÷ Total Gross Revenue) × 100**

### The Valuation Multiple Impact:
Most small-to-mid market corporate acquisitions are valued as a direct multiple of EBITDA (typically **4x to 8x EBITDA**).

| Financial Performance | Company A (Unoptimized Retention) | Company B (Lean Retention & Operations) |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Annual Gross Revenue | $5,000,000 | $5,000,000 |
| Operating Costs & Turnover Friction | −$4,500,000 | −$4,100,000 |
| **Generated EBITDA** | **$500,000 (10% Margin)** | **$900,000 (18% Margin)** |
| Valuation at 5x Multiple | **$2,500,000 Enterprise Value** | **$4,500,000 Enterprise Value** |
| **Owner Equity Difference** | Baseline | **+$2,000,000 in Enterprise Wealth!** |

💰 **Auditor's Insight:** Recovering 8 percentage points of EBITDA margin on identical revenue creates **$2 million in additional equity value** at exit. Operational cost control is the single fastest wealth creator in business.

## Metric 4: Current Ratio (Short-Term Liquidity Health)

Your **Current Ratio** informs credit underwriters and vendors whether your business can satisfy its impending obligations over the next 12 months using short-term assets.

### The Current Ratio Formula

> **Current Ratio = Total Current Assets ÷ Total Current Liabilities**

- **Current Assets:** Cash in bank, marketable securities, trade accounts receivable (AR), and liquid raw material inventory.
- **Current Liabilities:** Trade accounts payable (AP), short-term bank credit lines, accrued payroll, and current debt maturities.

| Current Ratio Score | Financial Health Rating | Lender Perception & Underwriting Action |
|:---:|:---:|---|
| **Below 1.0** | **🔴 Critical Risk** | Insolvent short-term; commercial loan applications denied immediately |
| **1.0 to 1.4** | **🟠 Vulnerable** | Tight liquidity; high interest rates and personal guarantees demanded |
| **1.5 to 2.2** | **🟢 Healthy Benchmark** | Ideal working capital balance; standard loan approval terms |
| **Above 3.0** | **🔵 Inefficient Capital** | Excessive cash sitting idle; failure to reinvest in growth assets |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do you accurately convert an hourly wage to an annual salary?
The standard baseline formula assumes a 40-hour work week across 52 weeks: Hourly Rate × 2,080 = Annual Gross Salary. However, when comparing contractors to salaried employees, add 25% to 35% for employer burden (payroll taxes, health insurance, paid leave, and retirement contributions).

### What is considered a healthy employee turnover rate?
Across professional services and technology, an annual turnover rate below 12% to 15% is considered healthy. High-attrition industries like retail or hospitality commonly average 50% to 70%. Any company running 5 percentage points above its specific industry benchmark faces severe hidden cost leakage.

### What is a good EBITDA margin for an established business?
An EBITDA margin of 15% to 25% is considered healthy across most sectors. Software and SaaS firms often exceed 30%, professional consulting achieves 20% to 30%, and manufacturing typically ranges from 12% to 18%. Margins below 10% signal structural overhead issues.

### What current ratio do commercial bank lenders require for loans?
Commercial lenders generally require a minimum current ratio of 1.5, with 2.0 considered the optimal safety benchmark. A current ratio below 1.0 indicates that short-term liabilities exceed short-term liquid assets, triggering immediate loan denial.

### How does employee turnover directly compress EBITDA margins?
Replacing a departed employee costs between 50% and 150% of their annual salary in recruiter fees, management interview hours, sign-on bonuses, and lost operational productivity. These cash drains hit operating expenses (OPEX) directly, compressing your EBITDA margin.
